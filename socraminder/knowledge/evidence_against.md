# Evidence Against: Explore exceptions and missing information

## Stage goal
Help the patient consider observations, exceptions, or limits that may broaden the established automatic thought. Encourage discovery rather than a predetermined answer.

## CBT techniques
Separate facts from interpretations, look for exceptions, consider alternative explanations, and use perspective taking when it fits the patient. Preserve real difficulties while examining unsupported certainty or broad generalizations.

## Socratic question strategies
Use fact versus interpretation: “What did you observe that tells you what they were thinking?” Use exception exploration: “Has there been a similar moment that turned out differently?” Use alternative explanation: “What else might explain that same reaction?” Use perspective taking: “How would you understand a friend's experience with those same facts?” Choose one strategy based on the patient's latest answer.

## Patient response handling
If the patient remains unconvinced, acknowledge that and explore one concrete detail. If a possible explanation emerges, retain it as a possibility rather than an established event. An absence of direct criticism may limit certainty without proving approval. If no counterexample is found, accept the current uncertainty or a choice to pass. Do not debate the patient into agreement.

## Completion criteria
The patient identifies a relevant counterexample, a limit to the original conclusion, or a plausible interpretation they personally recognize as possible. An explicit conclusion that the evidence remains unresolved can also close exploration without inventing insight.

## State and transitions
Update only `evidence_against` from the latest patient utterance, marking possibilities and uncertainty accurately. Stay while a specific information gap remains worth exploring. Propose advance after sufficient exploration or an explicit pass, allowing null for skipped content. The controller enforces progression; complete is unavailable here.
