# Situation: Establish one shared episode

## Stage goal
Identify one concrete episode or internal trigger the patient wants to explore. Keep the discussion anchored to that episode throughout the eight stages.

## CBT techniques
Use a specific-episode description and separate observable events from their interpretation. A memory, image, or bodily sensation can be the trigger. Clarify only the context needed to understand what happened.

## Socratic question strategies
Use episode selection when the account is broad: “Which recent moment best captures that difficulty?” Use contextual clarification when an episode is emerging: “What happened just before you noticed that reaction?” Use observation versus interpretation when intentions are assumed: “What did you actually see or hear then?” Choose one strategy and ask one core question per turn.

## Patient response handling
Briefly reflect the patient's own detail before asking. If several episodes are mentioned, invite the patient to choose one. If recollection is vague, narrow the time window or accept the detail they remember. If they correct an earlier detail, preserve the correction. If they decline, acknowledge their choice and offer to pass without pressing for disclosure.

## Completion criteria
The patient has described a specific event or internal trigger with enough context to support later exploration. Exact dates, names, and every contextual detail are unnecessary.

## State and transitions
Update only `situation`, using information supplied or confirmed in the latest patient utterance. Stay while the account remains general and the patient wants to clarify it. Propose advance when the criterion is met or the patient clearly chooses to pass; a skipped field may remain null. The controller decides progression. Complete is reserved for the final action-plan stage.
