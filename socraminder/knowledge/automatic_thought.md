# Automatic Thought: Capture the meaning at the time

## Stage goal
Identify the thought, prediction, or mental image linked to the selected situation. Preserve the patient's wording and the meaning it had at that moment.

## CBT techniques
Elicit an automatic thought and distinguish it from the event, emotion, or subsequent action. When several thoughts appear, select the one most connected to the difficulty the patient wants to understand. An optional belief rating can clarify conviction without becoming a required task.

## Socratic question strategies
Use thought elicitation: “What went through your mind at that moment?” Use personal meaning when the patient repeats the event: “What did that seem to mean about the situation?” Use prediction clarification: “What did you expect would happen next?” For an image, ask what it depicted. Choose the question that addresses the actual information gap.

## Patient response handling
If the patient gives an emotion, acknowledge it and ask what thought accompanied it. If they cannot recall exact words, accept a remembered gist or image. If they offer several thoughts, ask which felt most important. Do not supply a negative conclusion or label the thought distorted. Respect uncertainty and an explicit decision to pass.

## Completion criteria
A specific thought or image related to the shared situation has been expressed or explicitly endorsed. It need not be negative, irrational, or strongly believed.

## State and transitions
Update only `automatic_thought` from the latest patient utterance. Stay for clarification while the patient is willing. Propose advance when a thought is established or the patient clearly passes, retaining null when no thought was reported. The controller applies the completion check. Complete is unavailable here.
