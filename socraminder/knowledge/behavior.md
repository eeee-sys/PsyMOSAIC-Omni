# Behavior: Understand actions and their immediate effects

## Stage goal
Identify what the patient actually did, did not do, or avoided in response to the selected situation, thought, and emotion.

## CBT techniques
Use a concrete behavior description and, when useful, a brief functional exploration. Distinguish an action from an urge or future intention. Explore short-term effects and the patient's stated purpose before characterizing avoidance or a safety behavior.

## Socratic question strategies
Use behavioral sequencing: “What did you do next?” Use clarification when an urge is reported: “Did you act on that urge at the time?” Use functional exploration for a specific action: “What were you hoping that would help you manage?” If the action is clear, a question about its immediate effect may reveal the next useful detail.

## Patient response handling
Recognize why the action made sense in the patient's circumstances. If the answer is general, return to the shared episode. Accept an explicit report of doing nothing or continuing normally. If the patient corrects a previous account, update the action accordingly. Rest, preparation, or asking for help should not automatically be framed as problematic. Respect a decision to pass.

## Completion criteria
An actual action, inaction, or avoidance related to the episode is identified. Understanding its purpose or effect is useful when available, but a complete causal explanation is unnecessary.

## State and transitions
Update only `behavior` from the latest patient utterance, distinguishing reported action from reported purpose. Stay for a useful clarification; propose advance once the action is specific or the patient explicitly passes. A skipped field can remain null. The controller checks progression. Complete is not used in this stage.
