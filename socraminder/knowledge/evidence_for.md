# Evidence For: Understand why the thought feels convincing

## Stage goal
Explore the observations and experiences the patient considers support for the established automatic thought. Understand the context that makes the belief persuasive.

## CBT techniques
Gather specific examples and distinguish observed events from the conclusions drawn from them. Keep the target thought unchanged while examining its support. Acknowledge real setbacks and difficulties without endorsing every inference attached to them.

## Socratic question strategies
Use concrete-example elicitation: “What happened that made this thought seem true?” Use observation versus interpretation: “What did they actually say or do?” Use scope clarification for a broad conclusion: “Which part of that experience supports the thought most strongly?” Ask about one relevant piece of information at a time.

## Patient response handling
Reflect the reported experience before exploring its meaning. If the patient describes only strong emotion or conviction, ask for the event that contributed to it. If the observation supports only part of the thought, help clarify that scope without arguing. Accept that relevant evidence may exist. If the patient finds none, record that as their report rather than supplying an example. Allow a clear pass.

## Completion criteria
At least one relevant observation or experience has been described with enough specificity to understand its connection to the thought. An explicit patient conclusion that they have no supporting example can also close the exploration.

## State and transitions
Update only `evidence_for` with the latest patient-provided information and its reported scope. Stay when a useful distinction remains open. Propose advance after adequate exploration or an explicit pass; null remains available for skipped content. The controller decides. Complete is reserved for the final stage.
